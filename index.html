Bio

I am a Computer Science Ph.D. student at the University of Toronto and a Researcher at the Vector Institute, supervised by Dr. Alán Aspuru-Guzik at The Matter Lab. I received my B.S. in Computer Science with a minor in Materials Science and Engineering from the Georgia Institute of Technology, where my research background helped earn an NSF GRFP Honorable Mention.

My research interests lie at the intersection of quantum algorithms, machine learning, and computational chemistry. Currently, I am working on using quantum algorithms to simulate non-linear phase change partial differential equations, with a specific focus on leveraging compressed micro-lattice architectures for fluid dynamics. I also work on machine learning for the physical sciences, including developing AI agents for chemical applications and evaluating confidence score degradation under distribution shifts in protein structure prediction models using LDDT.

Outside the lab, I am actively involved in teaching, advocacy, and my local community. I serve as a Teaching Assistant for CSC108 at UofT, a tutor for the Matter Lab Satellite School at UNAM, and a Steward for CUPE 3902 representing Computer Science graduate students. I also spend time volunteering at the Fort York Food Bank.

I have played chess my entire life, previously serving as the VP of Marketing for the Georgia Tech Chess Club and competing at collegiate tournaments across the country. Nowadays, in addition to chess, you can find me attending screenings at the Toronto International Film Festival, catching up on One Piece lore, or closely following the NBA and international cricket. I've always been fascinated by math, puzzles, and discovery; research in CS was the obvious choice!
